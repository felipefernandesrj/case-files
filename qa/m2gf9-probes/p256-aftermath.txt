Write the "wrong verdict" aftermath stories for two detective cases in a browser game. When the player accuses an innocent suspect and gives up, the game shows a short newspaper-style consequence story: what happens to the innocent person who was wrongly accused, and how the real culprit gets away with it.

Case "violin" (The Vanishing Violin, Aurora Music Academy). Real culprit: Carlos Mendes, janitor, stole the Stradivarius to pay for his daughter's surgery and hid it in his cleaning cart. Innocent suspects: lorenzo = Lorenzo Moretti (conductor), maya = Maya Santos (violin prodigy).
Case "night-train" (The Night Train, overnight train Southern Star, between Alder Crossing and Port Elling). Real culprit: Inês Duarte, dining car chef, took the Duchess's sapphire necklace during the tunnel blackout with a copied key and hid it in a flour tin. Innocent suspects: helena = Helena Costa (train conductor), victor = Victor Hale (jeweller, passenger), tomas = Tomás Reyes (night porter).

Rules for every story:
- English "en" and Brazilian Portuguese "pt" with the same content.
- "headline": a newspaper headline, max 8 words, no final period.
- "body": exactly 3 sentences, 45 to 70 words in total. Sentence 1: the consequence for the wrongly accused person (specific to their job). Sentence 2: the real culprit quietly gets away, with one concrete detail from the case (the cart, the flour tin, the station). Sentence 3: a lasting consequence that makes the player want to try again.
- Noir tone, no gore, no em-dash or en-dash characters, no semicolons, plain ASCII quotes.
- Never reveal the motive word for word.
